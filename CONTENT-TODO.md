## 3. Quotations

### Checked against the official text (Oct 24, 2026)

Catechism: every quotation on the site was rechecked word for word against the Catechism text in the **Lumina** repository (`munkachy/lumina`), as well as vatican.va. CCC 11, 28, 30 (Augustine, *Confessions* 1.1), 80, 108, 122, 124, 129, 133, 149, 156, 818, 819, 838, 882, 891 (paraphrased), 956, 971, 1175, 1178, 1366, 1367, 1441, 1996, 2010, 2011 (St. Thérèse), 2132, 2585, and (new) 169, 1670, 1671.

**Corrections found while checking:**

- CCC 818 says the Church "**accepts** them with respect and affection as brothers," not "accepted." (The handoff file had "accepted.") The site quotes the second sentence of 818, which is the clearest: "they therefore have a right to be called Christians, and with good reason are accepted as brothers in the Lord by the children of the Catholic Church."
- CCC 108: the current (second-edition) text in Lumina reads “not a written and mute word, but **the Word which is** incarnate and living.” The older vatican.va text lacks “the Word which is.” The site now uses the newer wording.
- CCC 108 doesn't name St. Bernard in the text itself. The quoted words come from him, as the Catechism's footnote shows (*S. missus est hom.* 4, 11).

Other documents (official text):

- *Sacrosanctum Concilium* 51, 83 and 84; *Dei Verbum* 21; *Lumen Gentium* 8: vatican.va.
- GILH 12: checked against the text published by the Liturgy Office of England and Wales. **Note:** the US (ICEL) translation words it slightly differently ("the praise and thanksgiving, the memorial of the mysteries of salvation, the petitions, and the foretaste of heavenly glory"). If you'd rather quote the US text, I need a copy of it to check against.
- Benedict XVI, General Audience, June 22, 2011 (the Psalms as "the book of prayer"): vatican.va.
- Leo XIV, *Magnifica Humanitas* 9 and 114 (May 15, 2026): vatican.va.
- USCCB, *Guidelines for the Reception of Communion*: usccb.org.
- Every Bible quotation: exact text of the World English Bible (Catholic edition, eBible.org, public domain). Psalm titles are left out.

## 4. Things I decided (easy to change)

- **Translation:** World English Bible, **Catholic edition** (eBible.org). It prints "the LORD," not "Yahweh," in line with the Church's 2008 instruction not to use the divine name in worship. It is public domain.
- **Your heard replies now follow the Grail wording again** (Oct 24): "ten-stringed lyre … murmuring harp" (Ps 92), "spent and crushed" (Ps 38), "raise the cup of salvation" (Ps 116), "I am with you" (Ps 91), and so on. Verse numbers in the meditations follow the Grail book (older numbering, titles counted).
- **Psalm 27 (26):8 in the Grail** has the heart repeat "Seek his face" rather than God saying "Seek my face." The commentary on `meditations/` and `seekers/` was reworded to fit, with a note that many Bibles put the words in God's mouth.
- **Who is speaking:** a small round portrait beside each line. "I hear God say": the 6th-century Christ Pantocrator icon from Saint Catherine's Monastery, Sinai. "I pray": Memling's Saint Benedict (1487), a Benedictine in a black habit with his book.
- **Meditations page (Oct 24, round 3):** a mood picker at the top ("What kind of mood are you in right now?") with eleven boxes, each jumping to its call. Each verse and its reply now share one box. The numbering explanation moved to `our-psalter/#numbering`, with a one-line pointer left on the meditations page.
- **One verse, one voice (Oct 24, round 5):** every meditation now alternates the soul ("I pray") and God ("I hear God say"), one voice per verse, and the replies put things in fresh words instead of repeating the psalm. Psalms 38, 42, 91, 46, 32, 131, 116, and 27 were reworked; 102 and 92 were left as Br. Augustine approved them (102:3 still has two voices on one verse). The door-page examples match.
- **Tips (Oct 24):** eleven short tips, "For Dummies" style, one after each meditation, all gathered on `meditations/tips/`. The first, "What if I get stuck?", is Br. Augustine's "static on the line" idea. The rest are mine; edit freely.
- **Quotes (Oct 24):** eight quotes about prayer, each in a "Quote" box where it fits (home: St. Teresa; Times: "He who sings prays twice"; Our book: CCC 2585; meditations: St. Thérèse, CCC 2560, the farmer of Ars, CCC 2559, St. Gregory of Nazianzus), all gathered on `quotes/`. Every Catechism quotation was checked word for word against the Lumina Catechism, except the Thérèse line (see above).
- **New tip, "What if I'm really tired?"** (after Psalm 131): one short word, from *The Cloud of Unknowing*. A "Tired" mood box now leads there too.
- **Stained glass theme uses the abbey's own glass (Oct 24):** the strip under the header and over the footer is two of the narrow mosaic windows, photographed and laid on their side. Quote boxes sit on the rough glass blocks. The round theme button is a piece of the same glass. Only visitors who choose that theme download these pictures.
- **New name (Oct 24, final): "The Oldest Conversation."** Chosen over "Conversations with God" (already the title of a well-known New Age book series). The opening paragraph says the Psalms have been sung to God for about three thousand years, and Benedictine monks have kept that up for some fifteen hundred. "Praying for the whole world, and for you" stays in the opening paragraph. Earlier name tried: "We're Here, Praying for You." Chosen so the first thing a visitor reads is a gift, not a request. Under it: "Nothing to join. Nothing to pay. Nothing you have to believe first." The doors now sit under "Here's how you can join in," with two buttons below them: "Pray a psalm now" and "Come to a prayer time." In running text, "sing with us" became "pray with us." The web address still says `sing-with-us`; change it (or buy a domain) before printing QR signs.
- **Home page top (Oct 24, later):** "It's a song that has never stopped. You can sing along, or just sit and listen." Then "No membership. No fees. No strings. Nothing you have to believe first." and Matthew 10:8, "Freely you received, so freely give" (WEB, checked in Lumina). The doors heading is now "Would you like to join in?"
