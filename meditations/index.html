Picking up the phone

Meditations

One monk's conversations with God in the Psalms

David composing the Psalms, Paris Psalter, about 960, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS gr. 139 (photo: Gennadii Saus i Segura) · public domain

These are examples of one way to pray the Psalms. In each one, I hear one verse as my own voice and the next as God answering me. Every call is different: one day sorrowful, the next full of joy.

Please read this first

  • These are personal meditations by a Benedictine monk. They are not Church teaching, and they are not part of the Bible.
  • The psalm text is never changed. It is shown in the shaded boxes, quoted exactly from the World English Bible, a public-domain translation. (Our choir sings the Grail translation, which is under copyright.)
  • The heard reply is set apart in italics below each verse, marked “I pray” or “I hear.” It isn't a new translation, and it isn't a claim about what the psalm's writer meant. Hebrew poetry likes to speak in pairs, and the second line often deepens the first. That pairing simply invites us to listen for an answer.

Two ways of numbering the Psalms

Most Bibles today number the Psalms from the Hebrew text. Older Bibles, made from the Greek and Latin, count some psalms together or split them, so the numbers differ. Our choir book uses the older numbering. For most psalms from 10 to 146, the older number is one lower. On this site we give the number in most Bibles first, then the older number in parentheses: Psalm 92 (91).

Verse numbers can also differ by one or two between translations, because some count a psalm's title as verse 1. The verse numbers here follow the World English Bible.

A sorrowful call

Psalm 102 (101)

Interceding for the dying

Hear my prayer, LORD! Let my cry come to you.

Psalm 102:1Exact WEB text

I hearI'm listening to your prayer. Your cry has reached me. What do you ask of me?

Don't hide your face from me in the day of my distress. Turn your ear to me. Answer me quickly in the day when I call.

Psalm 102:2Exact WEB text

I prayYou did not hide your face from me when I was in distress. Now others are in distress, and I want to pray for them.

I hearMy ear is turned toward you. I will answer you quickly.

For my days consume away like smoke. My bones are burned as a torch.

Psalm 102:3Exact WEB text

I prayLook at the dying all over the world. Their days are vanishing like smoke.

My heart is blighted like grass, and withered, for I forget to eat my bread.

Psalm 102:4Exact WEB text

I hearYes. Some of them are so worn out that they forget to eat.

By reason of the voice of my groaning, my bones stick to my skin.

Psalm 102:5Exact WEB text

I prayGive them strength to cry out to you, even when their bones stick to their skin.

In this prayer I let some of the psalm's own “I” lines become God's voice, and I turn others into prayers for people who are dying. That is the freedom of a meditation. The psalm itself still says exactly what it says.

A joyful call

Psalm 92 (91)

Our community prays this psalm at Lauds on Saturday morning.

It is a good thing to give thanks to the LORD, to sing praises to your name, Most High,

Psalm 92:1Exact WEB text

I hearIt is good to give thanks to me, and to make music to me, the Most High.

to proclaim your loving kindness in the morning, and your faithfulness every night,

Psalm 92:2Exact WEB text

I prayThen I will proclaim your love this morning, and your faithfulness again tonight.

with the ten-stringed lute, with the harp, and with the melody of the lyre.

Psalm 92:3Exact WEB text

I hearI've given you your ten-stringed lute: the music program you're building. And your harp: the looper you're working on.

For you, LORD, have made me glad through your work. I will triumph in the works of your hands.

Psalm 92:4Exact WEB text

I prayI'm the one building these things, but really it's your hands building them through me. And I shout for joy.

How great are your works, LORD! Your thoughts are very deep.

Psalm 92:5Exact WEB text

I hearYes. Aren't my works great? My thoughts are so deep that I can work right inside you.

One day I prayed Psalm 102 for the dying. Another day I prayed this one, full of joy. Same monk, same choir, a completely different call.

An honest call

Psalm 38 (37)

On a gloomy day, for no good reason. This was the first psalm I prayed this way.

My wounds are loathsome and corrupt because of my foolishness.

Psalm 38:5Exact WEB text

I prayLord, I'm gloomy today, and I don't even know why. I can't see any of the good things you've given me.

I am in pain and bowed down greatly. I go mourning all day long.

Psalm 38:6Exact WEB text

I hearI see you bowed down before me. I see you mourning all day long. I am the Good Physician, and I'm going to help you get better.

For my waist is filled with burning. There is no soundness in my flesh.

Psalm 38:7Exact WEB text

I prayThen heal me. I'm burning with a fever, the fever of ingratitude. It's like a fever dream: everything seems wrong, when really I'm surrounded by blessings.

I am faint and severely bruised. I have groaned by reason of the anguish of my heart.

Psalm 38:8Exact WEB text

I hearI see that you are worn out and bruised. I hear you groaning.

Lord, all my desire is before you. My groaning is not hidden from you.

Psalm 38:9Exact WEB text

I prayYes, Lord, you see it all. You've given me life and everything good, and still these feelings come. I'm not hiding them from you.

A gloomy mood isn't always ingratitude. Sometimes it is an illness, and that is neither a sin nor a sign of holiness. If sadness won't lift, please talk to a doctor or counselor as well as a priest. The Psalms can keep you company on that road too.

When your own heart talks back

Psalm 42 (41)

Why are you in despair, my soul? Why are you disturbed within me? Hope in God! For I shall still praise him for the saving help of his presence.

Psalm 42:5Exact WEB text

Here the conversation happens inside one person. The psalmist hears his own voice sinking, and he lifts it. Question, then answer. Sinking, then rising. Sometimes the first voice that answers you is your own soul, remembering who God is.

A call God finishes

Psalm 91 (90)

I will say of the LORD, “He is my refuge and my fortress; my God, in whom I trust.”

Psalm 91:2Exact WEB text

The psalm begins with the soul pledging its trust. At the end, God himself speaks (verses 14–16):

He will call on me, and I will answer him. I will be with him in trouble. I will deliver him, and honor him.

Psalm 91:15Exact WEB text

I prayYou are my refuge. You are my fortress. I trust you.

I hearI will answer you. I will be with you in trouble. And then, quietly: I'm with you now.

For anxious days

Psalm 46 (45)

God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore we won't be afraid, though the earth changes, though the mountains are shaken into the heart of the seas;

Psalm 46:1–2Exact WEB text

Then, near the end, God himself speaks:

“Be still, and know that I am God.”

Psalm 46:10Exact WEB text

I prayLord, the world is shaking.

I hearTurn it off for a minute. Be still. I am God, and I haven't left.

Confession, and the answer

Psalm 32 (31)

I acknowledged my sin to you. I didn't hide my iniquity. I said, I will confess my transgressions to the LORD, and you forgave the iniquity of my sin.

Psalm 32:5Exact WEB text

Notice how fast it happens: I said, I will confess … and you forgave. No waiting room. No file. And a few verses later, God himself speaks:

I will instruct you and teach you in the way which you shall go. I will counsel you with my eye on you.

Psalm 32:8Exact WEB text

When you're afraid God is keeping score

Psalm 130 (129)

If you, LORD, kept a record of sins, Lord, who could stand?

Psalm 130:3Exact WEB text

I prayLord, if you're keeping score, I'm finished.

But there is forgiveness with you, therefore you are feared.

Psalm 130:4Exact WEB text

I hearI'm not keeping score. I'm forgiving. That's who I am.

The fear in the first verse is real, but the second verse has the last word. And “feared” here means awe, the reverence a child has for a loving father, not terror. “There is no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear” (1 John 4:18).Exact WEB text

The little way

Psalm 131 (130)

LORD, my heart isn't arrogant, nor my eyes lofty; nor do I concern myself with great matters, or things too wonderful for me.

Psalm 131:1Exact WEB text

Surely I have stilled and quieted my soul, like a weaned child with his mother, like a weaned child is my soul within me.

Psalm 131:2Exact WEB text

I hearI have stilled and quieted your soul. Rest here, like a child with its mother.

The soul has just admitted that it is small, and God answers in the psalm's own words, turned toward me. This is Saint Thérèse's “little way” in two verses. Being small before God isn't a demotion. It's an embrace.

A grateful call

Psalm 116 (115 in the older numbering, which splits this psalm in two)

What will I give to the LORD for all his benefits toward me?

Psalm 116:12Exact WEB text

I prayWhat can I give you, Lord, for all your goodness to me?

I will take the cup of salvation, and call on the LORD's name.

Psalm 116:13Exact WEB text

I hearHere's what you can do. Take the cup of salvation, and call on my name.

I prayReally? That's all?

I hearThat's all.

We'd often rather do something heroic than something simple. But the cure for ingratitude is simple. Lift the cup. Say thank you.

Two voices in one verse

Psalm 27 (26)

When you said, “Seek my face,” my heart said to you, “I will seek your face, LORD.”

Psalm 27:8Exact WEB text

This verse already holds the whole conversation. God speaks first, inviting: Seek my face. The heart answers, burning: I will seek your face. That is the whole Christian life in one breath. God has already made the call. The only question left is how your heart will answer.

Try it yourself

This is not a technique with steps. It's more like a style, a way of listening that becomes your own. Pick a psalm. Read one verse as your voice. Then read the next, and listen for how God might be saying it to you. Don't change the words. Just listen.

And when you're ready, come and pray them with us.
