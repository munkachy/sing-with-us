Tips for the Conversation

Tip

What if I get stuck?

Sometimes the line has static on it. You listen for God's answer, and nothing comes, or it comes through garbled. That's normal. Saint Paul says so:

For now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I will know fully, even as I was also fully known.

1 Corinthians 13:12, World English BibleCheck: matches the WEB (British edition) in Lumina; confirm against the Catholic edition

(The old King James Bible says we see “through a glass, darkly.”) In this life we really can talk with God, but not yet face to face. A little static comes with being a pilgrim.

That's the great gift of chanting together. When you get stuck, the choir keeps moving and carries you along to the next verse. So no big deal. Keep moving through the Psalms. Little by little they become part of you, and the line gets clearer. Some static will always be there until we see him face to face, and that's all right.

Tip

What if the psalm doesn't match my mood?

Some days the psalm is dancing and you feel like a wet rag. Other days you're happy and the psalm is weeping. Pray it anyway, for someone else. Somewhere, right now, someone feels exactly what this psalm feels. Lend them your voice. That's a big part of what monks do all day: we pray in the name of people who can't.

Tip

Do I have to feel something?

No. Feelings are like weather: they come and go. Some days prayer is warm, and some days it's as dry as dust. God doesn't measure your prayer by how it feels. Showing up is the prayer. Keep showing up.

Tip

What if my mind wanders?

It will. Everyone's does, monks included. When you notice you've drifted, don't scold yourself. Just come back at the next verse. Every verse is a new door, and coming back is part of the prayer.

Tip

How do I know it's God and not just me?

Honestly, you can't always be sure. These meditations aren't visions or new messages from heaven. They're a way of praying with the imagination God gave you. Here's a good test: Does what you hear agree with the Bible? Does it make you more loving, more humble, more at peace? Then keep listening. If it makes you proud, harsh, or afraid, it isn't from God. Let it go, and keep praying.

Tip

How much should I pray?

Start small. One verse is enough. Read it slowly, listen, answer, and stop. A short prayer prayed with your whole heart is worth more than a long one rushed. You can always come back tomorrow.

Tip

Can I tell God the hard stuff?

Yes. The Psalms do. They complain, they grieve, they ask God where he went. God put those prayers in the Bible on purpose. He would rather have your honest anger than your polite silence. Tell him what's really there. (Catholics can also bring their sins to confession and hear the words of forgiveness out loud.)

Tip

What if I'm not good enough to pray?

You're in good company. Many of the Psalms come down to us under the name of King David, who committed adultery and murder, then repented with his whole heart. The Psalms were written by sinners, for sinners. God doesn't wait for you to clean up before he picks up the phone. Come as you are. He does the cleaning.

Tip

What about the quiet in between?

Silence is part of the conversation. Between verses, don't rush to fill the quiet. Sometimes God's answer isn't words at all, just peace, like a child resting in its mother's arms. In choir there's a short pause in the middle of every verse. Let it be a breath for listening.

Tip

What if I'm really tired?

Then make it simple. Boil the whole prayer down to one word. As you chant or read, listen for one short word from God, like love or God. One syllable is best. Hold on to that word. When your mind drifts, it brings you back. When you have people to pray for, tuck them all inside that one word and offer it up.

This comes from a mysterious little book called The Cloud of Unknowing. It was written in English in the late 1300s by someone who never signed it, and to this day nobody knows who the author was. The book teaches that one little word, like God or love, can carry your whole heart to God. For more than six hundred years it has quietly guided monks, nuns, and ordinary people.Check: Cloud of Unknowing, ch. 7 (the one-syllable word, “God” or “love”)

It sounds almost too simple. It is simple. But it's powerful, and the longer you use it, the more you find inside that one word.

Tip

How do I keep it going after prayer?

Take one line with you. Pick a verse, or an answer you heard, and carry it through the day. Say it while you wait in line, drive, or wash dishes. Monks have done this for centuries. It turns a whole day into a quiet conversation.

Tip

Do I have to do this alone?

No, and it's easier together. In choir you don't carry the psalm by yourself. One side sings, the other answers, and the whole church moves as one. Come and pray with us. Your presence helps carry us, and we will help carry you. See the prayer times.
