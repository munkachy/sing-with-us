Meditations

One monk's conversations with God in the Psalms

David composing the Psalms, Paris Psalter, about 960, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS gr. 139 (photo: Gennadii Saus i Segura) · public domain

These are examples of one way to pray the Psalms. In each one, I hear one verse as my own voice and the next as God answering me. Every call is different: one day sorrowful, the next full of joy.

What kind of mood are you in right now?

Tap one, and you'll go straight to a call for that kind of day.

Please read this first

  • These are personal meditations by a Benedictine monk. They are not Church teaching, and they are not part of the Bible.
  • The psalm text is never changed. It is shown in plain type in the shaded boxes, in short excerpts from the Grail translation. That's the translation our choir sings, so these are the very words you'll hear in church.
  • The heard reply is set apart in italics, in the same box as its verse: beside it on a wide screen. On a phone, tap the box to open the meditation. A small picture shows who is speaking: an ancient icon of Christ for “I hear God say,” and a Benedictine monk in his black habit for “I pray.” It isn't a new translation, and it isn't a claim about what the psalm's writer meant. Hebrew poetry likes to speak in pairs, and the second line often deepens the first. That pairing simply invites us to listen for an answer.

A sorrowful call

Psalm 102 (101)

Interceding for the dying

O Lord, listen to my prayer
and let my cry for help reach you.

Psalm 102 (101):2Exact Grail text

I hear God sayI'm listening to your prayer. Your cry has reached me. What do you ask of me?

Do not hide your face from me
in the day of my distress.
Turn your ear towards me
and answer me quickly when I call.

Psalm 102 (101):3Exact Grail text

I prayYou did not hide your face from me when I was in distress. Now others are in distress, and I want to pray for them.

I hear God sayMy ear is turned toward you. I will answer you quickly.

For my days are vanishing like smoke,
my bones burn away like a fire.

Psalm 102 (101):4Exact Grail text

I prayLook at the dying all over the world. Their days are vanishing like smoke.

My heart is withered like the grass.
I forget to eat my bread.

Psalm 102 (101):5Exact Grail text

I hear God sayYes, my son. Some of them are so worn out that they forget to eat.

I cry with all my strength
and my skin clings to my bones.

Psalm 102 (101):6Exact Grail text

I prayGive them strength to cry out to you, even when their skin clings to their bones.

In this prayer I let some of the psalm's own “I” lines become God's voice, and I turn others into prayers for people who are dying. That is the freedom of a meditation. The psalm itself still says exactly what it says.

A joyful call

Psalm 92 (91)

Our community prays this psalm at Lauds on Saturday morning.

It is good to give thanks to the Lord,
to make music to your name, O Most High,

Psalm 92 (91):2Exact Grail text

I hear God sayIt is good to give thanks to me, and to make music to my name. I am the Most High.

to proclaim your love in the morning
and your truth in the watches of the night,

Psalm 92 (91):3Exact Grail text

I prayThen I will proclaim your love this morning, and your truth again tonight.

on the ten-stringed lyre and the lute,
with the murmuring sound of the harp.

Psalm 92 (91):4Exact Grail text

I hear God sayI've given you your ten-stringed lyre: the music program you're building. And your murmuring harp: the looper you're working on.

Your deeds, O Lord, have made me glad;
for the work of your hands I shout with joy.

Psalm 92 (91):5Exact Grail text

I prayI'm the one building these things, but really it's your hands building them through me. And I shout for joy.

O Lord, how great are your works!
How deep are your designs!

Psalm 92 (91):6Exact Grail text

I hear God sayYes. Aren't my works great? My designs are so deep that I can work right inside you.

One day I prayed Psalm 102 for the dying. Another day I prayed this one, full of joy. Same monk, same choir, a completely different call.

An honest call

Psalm 38 (37)

On a gloomy day, for no good reason. This was the first psalm I prayed this way.

My wounds are foul and festering,
the result of my own folly.

Psalm 38 (37):6Exact Grail text

I prayLord, I'm gloomy today, and I don't even know why. I can't see any of the good things you've given me.

I am bowed and brought to my knees.
I go mourning all the day long.

Psalm 38 (37):7Exact Grail text

I hear God sayI see you, my son, bowed down and brought to your knees. I see you mourning all the day long. I am the Good Physician, and I'm going to help you get better.

All my frame burns with fever;
all my body is sick.

Psalm 38 (37):8Exact Grail text

I prayThen heal me. I'm burning with a fever, the fever of ingratitude. It's like a fever dream: everything seems wrong, when really I'm surrounded by blessings.

Spent and utterly crushed,
I cry aloud in anguish of heart.

Psalm 38 (37):9Exact Grail text

I hear God sayI see that you are spent and crushed. I hear you crying out.

O Lord, you know all my longing:
my groans are not hidden from you.

Psalm 38 (37):10Exact Grail text

I prayYes, Lord, you know it all. You've given me life and everything good, and still these feelings come. I'm not hiding them from you.

A gloomy mood isn't always ingratitude. Sometimes it is an illness, and that is neither a sin nor a sign of holiness. If sadness won't lift, please talk to a doctor or counselor as well as a priest. The Psalms can keep you company on that road too.

When your own heart talks back

Psalm 42 (41)

Why are you cast down, my soul,
why groan within me?
Hope in God; I will praise him still,
my savior and my God.

Psalm 42 (41):6Exact Grail text

Here the conversation happens inside one person. The psalmist hears his own voice sinking, and he lifts it. Question, then answer. Sinking, then rising. Sometimes the first voice that answers you is your own soul, remembering who God is.

A call God finishes

Psalm 91 (90)

… says to the Lord: “My refuge,
my stronghold, my God in whom I trust!”

Psalm 91 (90):2Exact Grail text

The psalm begins with the soul pledging its trust. At the end, God himself speaks (verses 14–16):

When he calls I shall answer: “I am with you,”
I will save him in distress and give him glory.

Psalm 91 (90):15Exact Grail text

I prayYou are my refuge. You are my stronghold. I trust you.

I hear God sayWhen you call, I will answer: I am with you. And then, quietly: I'm with you now, my son.

For anxious days

Psalm 46 (45)

God is for us a refuge and strength,
a helper close at hand, in time of distress,
so we shall not fear though the earth should rock,
though the mountains fall into the depths of the sea;

Psalm 46 (45):2–3Exact Grail text

Then, near the end, God himself speaks:

“Be still and know that I am God,
supreme among the nations, supreme on the earth!”

Psalm 46 (45):11Exact Grail text

I prayLord, the earth is rocking.

I hear God sayTurn it off for a minute. Be still. I am God, and I haven't left.

Confession, and the answer

Psalm 32 (31)

But now I have acknowledged my sins;
my guilt I did not hide.
I said: “I will confess
my offense to the Lord.”
And you, Lord, have forgiven
the guilt of my sin.

Psalm 32 (31):5Exact Grail text

Notice how fast it happens: I said: “I will confess” … And you, Lord, have forgiven. No waiting room. No file. And a few verses later, God himself speaks:

I will instruct you and teach you
the way you should go;
I will give you counsel
with my eye upon you.

Psalm 32 (31):8Exact Grail text

When you're afraid God is keeping score

Psalm 130 (129)

If you, O Lord, should mark our guilt,
Lord, who would survive?

Psalm 130 (129):3Exact Grail text

I prayLord, if you're keeping score, I'm finished.

But with you is found forgiveness:
for this we revere you.

Psalm 130 (129):4Exact Grail text

I hear God sayI'm not keeping score. I'm forgiving. That's who I am.

The dread in the first verse is real, but the second verse has the last word. And “revere” here means awe, the reverence a child has for a loving father, not terror. “There is no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear” (1 John 4:18, World English Bible).Exact WEB text

The little way

Psalm 131 (130)

O Lord, my heart is not proud
nor haughty my eyes.
I have not gone after things too great
nor marvels beyond me.
Truly I have set my soul
in silence and peace.
A weaned child on its mother’s breast,
even so is my soul.

Psalm 131 (130):1–2Exact Grail text

I hear God sayI have set your soul in silence and peace. Rest here, like a weaned child on its mother's breast.

The soul has just admitted that it is small, and God answers in the psalm's own words, turned toward me. This is Saint Thérèse's “little way” in two verses. Being small before God isn't a demotion. It's an embrace.

A grateful call

Psalm 116 (115 in the older numbering, which splits this psalm in two)

How can I repay the Lord
for his goodness to me?

Psalm 116 (115):12Exact Grail text

I prayHow can I repay you, Lord, for all your goodness to me?

The cup of salvation I will raise;
I will call on the Lord’s name.

Psalm 116 (115):13Exact Grail text

I hear God sayHere's how. Raise the cup of salvation, and call on my name.

I prayReally? That's all?

I hear God sayThat's all, my son.

We'd often rather do something heroic than something simple. But the cure for ingratitude is simple. Lift the cup. Say thank you.

↑ Choose another mood

Two voices in one verse

Psalm 27 (26)

Of you my heart has spoken:
“Seek his face.”
It is your face, O Lord, that I seek;

Psalm 27 (26):8Exact Grail text

This verse already holds the whole conversation. The heart remembers God's own invitation and repeats it: Seek his face. Then it turns to God and answers, burning: It is your face, O Lord, that I seek. (Many Bibles put the invitation straight into God's mouth: “You said, ‘Seek my face.’”) That is the whole Christian life in one breath. God has already made the call. The only question left is how your heart will answer.

Try it yourself

This is not a technique with steps. It's more like a style, a way of listening that becomes your own. Pick a psalm. Read one verse as your voice. Then read the next, and listen for how God might be saying it to you. Don't change the words. Just listen.

And when you're ready, come and pray them with us.
