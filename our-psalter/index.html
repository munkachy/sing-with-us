Following along in our book

The choir and altar of the abbey church, Saint Bernard Abbey · © Saint Bernard Abbey

You don't need a book to pray with us. Listening is prayer too. But if you'd like to follow along, here's how our prayer works.

Our book

Our choir sings the psalms in English, from the Grail translation (the 1963 edition). The Grail was made to be sung, and its lines have a strong, steady rhythm.

Step 1: Know which hour it is

Each prayer time has a name. Here they are, in the order of the day:

  • Matins begins with “O Lord, open my lips, and my mouth shall proclaim your praise.” It continues with Psalm 94 (95 in most Bibles), a hymn, psalms with their antiphons, and readings.
  • Lauds, morning praise, follows right after Matins. It begins with “O God, come to my assistance.” There are two psalms with an Old Testament canticle between them, a short reading, a hymn, and the Canticle of Zechariah (the Benedictus).
  • Sext is the short midday prayer.
  • Vespers, evening prayer, has four psalms, each with its antiphon, and Mary's song, the Magnificat.
  • Compline, night prayer, begins with a blessing and a short reading. It ends with a song in honor of Mary.

This outline comes from the abbey's own page on the Divine Office. See the times.

Step 3: Find the psalm

Our book numbers the psalms the older way, from the Greek and Latin Bibles. Most Bibles today use the Hebrew numbering. For most psalms from 10 to 146, our number is one lower than in a modern Bible.

So Psalm 23, “The Lord is my shepherd,” is Psalm 22 in our book. Verse numbers can differ by one or two as well, because our book counts a psalm's title as verse 1.

On this website we give the number in most Bibles first, then the number in our book in parentheses: Psalm 92 (91).

Step 4: Listen for the antiphon

An antiphon is a short verse sung before (and often after) a psalm. It sets the theme, like a key signature in music. Listen for it, and it will tell you which psalm is coming.

Step 5: Follow the back-and-forth

Two monks, the chanters (a lead and a second), stand and give the note. Together they sing the first verse. Then the whole community answers with the next verse. Back and forth it goes, verse by verse, to the end of the psalm. Each psalm usually ends with “Glory be to the Father …”

Confirm: Does each psalm end with the Glory Be here? Any bowing, standing, or sitting guests should know?

Little by little, the pitch tends to sink. Then the chanters lift it again. If you're singing, listen to them, and rise with them.

Guests are welcome to sing along. Sing a little softer than the monks, stay with their pace, and pause where they pause. More tips for singing along.

Step 6: When to stand, sit, or bow

You don't need to know. Do what feels natural, or follow the people around you. Nobody is watching to see whether you get it right.

Lost your place? That's all right. Close the book and listen. You're still praying with us.
