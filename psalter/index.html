How to use this page
Choosing the book, the week, the day and the hour

The top row chooses the book: the Psalter, or the seasonal books for Advent and Christmas, Lent and Easter (with the feasts after Pentecost and the Sundays through the year), and the Saints. In a seasonal book, pick the section, then “Go to” an hour.

Tap the week the choir is on (I–IV). This phone remembers it and moves on every Sunday. Until you choose, the page follows the ordo's week, marked with a small dot under its button; tap the dotted week to follow the ordo again.

The p. button opens any page by the number printed in the book you're in; the faint number in the corner shows the page you're reading. Turn the phone sideways to hide the bar and see the page full width.

What opens by itself

The page opens at the hour being prayed now, or the next one, by the abbey's horarium. On a feast or solemnity in the ordo it opens that day's pages in the Saints or seasonal book instead (I Vespers the evening before), with a link back to the psalter.

On All Souls (2 November) it opens the Office of the Dead from the Saints book, its own Compline too (the necrology still follows), and on 14 November, the Departed of our Order, the same for Matins, Lauds and Sext (Vespers is I Vespers of Sunday). Notices from the Master of Ceremonies appear on the ordo line and in the calendar.

The calendar button by the themes shows the whole ordo; tap a day there to see its hours here.

The ordo line

Under the bar is the American-Cassinese Congregation's ordo for the day you've picked, with the lines for Saint Bernard Abbey and our diocese in bold, the day's Mass readings (read them at USCCB), and, on the anniversary of a brother of our house, the Necrology: tap it to read his notice.

How each hour is put together

Where the book prints a text for each season (Through the Year, Advent, Christmas, Lent, Holy Week, Paschaltide …), only the one for the day is shown. “Show the book pages instead” brings back the book exactly as printed.

Matins: Psalm 94 after the invitatory, in the invitatory's mode, as the cantors sing it; one hymn on weekdays (for darkness, or for light when dawn at the abbey comes before Matins; a link switches it); the day's two readings in their places; on Sundays and feasts a choice of Te Deum (English as printed, sung in Mode IV or VII, or Latin).

Lauds: the Benedictus sung in English (Mode 1) under its words. Sext: its opening, the hymn for the day and the antiphon, reading and versicle of the season. Saturdays: Matins and Lauds show the ordinary texts, or on the abbey's Saturdays of Our Lady only hers.

Vespers: on Saturday and Sunday evenings through the year, the psalter's Vespers with the Sunday's own Magnificat antiphon (by year), the Magnificat in its first alternate tone. Feasts in the Saints book are pasted together hour by hour: each antiphon with its psalm, the Gospel canticle and the Te Deum where the book points to them.

Compline: only tonight's hymn and the antiphon of Our Lady for the season (solemn tone on Sundays, solemnities and feasts). On Wednesday and Sunday, the haustus days, the Ultima instead; on a feast it is Compline, with a link to the Ultima if the Abbot keeps the haustus.

Book, Lectio and Cantus

Above the pages, choose how to read the hour. Book shows the book's own pages. Lectio sets everything as text, proofread from the book: rubrics in red, the psalms with their large capitals and their * and †, and the chant as its words. Cantus is the same text, but wherever the book has music its notation is kept. This phone remembers your choice.

The text is being proofread hour by hour; so far, Sunday Matins and Lauds of Week I, with Psalm 94 and the sung Benedictus. Where an hour isn't in text yet, its pages are shown as in the book. Marks with ✎ are made on the book's pages.

The hebdomadarian's Proper

The whole Liber Hebdomadarii, typed in its places: the capitulum under “Reading” at Lauds, Sext and Vespers, and the Oration under “Prayer from the Proper”. The page follows the year: Sundays and seasons, feasts and solemnities, the saints the abbey celebrates (a memorial on an ordinary weekday), Our Lady's Saturdays, and on the eve of a Sunday or solemnity its I Vespers. Weekdays through the year follow the psalter week; at Lauds they keep the Psalter's reading. For the weekday Oration choose “As printed in the Psalter” or “From the Sunday Proper” (that week's Sunday); where the book gives several Orations (Our Lady's Saturday has eight), numbered buttons choose one. This phone remembers your choices. Good Friday and Holy Saturday are not in the Liber.

The Matins readings

The Biblical and Patristic readings from the abbey's Two-Year Lectionary, typed in their places: the first under “First Reading”, the second under “Second Reading”, each before its responsory. Year I from Advent 2026. A feast or solemnity with its own readings takes them, and so do our own days (13 and 14 November) and All Souls; a memorial keeps the weekday's. Where a day has another reading, buttons above it let you switch.

At the end of the hour

After Compline or the Ultima comes the necrology: the brothers of Saint Bernard Abbey who died on that day, each with his notice from our necrology to read, and the rest of the Congregation. On the day of a Benedictine saint, the collect from the Benedictine Supplement follows Matins, Lauds and Vespers.

Your highlights, notes and marks

Select words in a reading and they are highlighted in gold; tap the highlight to add a note, choose one of four colours, or remove it. The ✎ button lets you drag a mark over part of a book page. They are kept only in this browser: nobody else sees them. The same button makes a backup file (to keep, or to read into another phone) and a readable copy to print.

This book was “printed not published,” for the monks, oblates, students and guests of Saint Bernard Abbey, “for private distribution only.” Please don't share the password or the pages outside the community.
